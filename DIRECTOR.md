# Direct the story

Start from a question the viewer can answer differently after watching.
Match the running time to the argument. Choose the audience, language and
voice from the request; ask only for choices that materially affect the film.

For factual stories, write a short source ledger before drawing. Keep study
populations, comparisons and limitations with the claims they qualify.

Use the shared methods for [reference adaptation](methods/reference-adaptation.md),
[media selection](methods/media-selection.md) and [revision delivery](methods/revision-delivery.md)
when those decisions arise. They apply across styles; set the medium, motion,
duration and verification requirements from this film's brief.

Read the selected style guide. Plan a few connected visual ideas, not a sequence
of decorated captions. People should do something: operate a tool, hesitate,
try again, or discover a result. Use recognizable objects and place cues when
they matter to the explanation. Do not add figures just to fill space.

Write narration that leaves room to see the action. Generate or import audio
before final timing. Choreograph drawing, camera moves, reading holds, sound
and transitions around one shared clock. Maintain character identity.

Render representative stills, then a short motion preview. Fix collisions,
illegible writing, disconnected pen strokes and awkward camera movement before
rendering the full film. Check the encoded result and actual playback. Keep
review observations specific; do not equate automated checks with watching
or listening. Iterate autonomously within the requested scope.
