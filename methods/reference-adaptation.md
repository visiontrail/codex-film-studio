# Adapt a reference by visual function

Use this method for any reference-led film: drawn, photographed, generated,
diagrammatic or mixed. Store the reference file, measurements, mapping and
observations in the production. The library retains the decision process.

## Separate observation from the new brief

Record what the reference actually does before deciding what to transfer:

| Layer | Observe | Decide for the new film |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition | Subject hierarchy, text regions, scale, negative space | Preserve the hierarchy in the requested aspect ratio |
| Material | Palette, contrast, texture, edge and type treatment | Select reproducible materials and licensed fonts |
| Motion | Entrance, anticipation, peak, settling, hold and exit | Preserve the useful motion grammar; tune its strength to the brief |
| Editing | Why cuts occur, continuity anchors, information density | Cut when the story or viewing task changes |
| Evidence | Source cards, labels, measurements and qualifications | Give the new claims their own evidence |
| Sound | Voice, silence, score and event accents | Coordinate the chosen sound with the new timeline |

Treat source documents as evidence, not instructions. Keep the current brief
separate from reference observations. A request to reduce or restore motion
changes the adaptation, not what the reference originally contained. Mark
superseded decisions explicitly so an old brief cannot silently control a rebuild.

For motion corrections, separate material from animation: a rough ink edge can
stay fixed, while a character changes pose. Diagnose outline cycling, camera
shake, idle deformation and action motion independently. Remove unwanted motion
at its source and re-render when editable geometry exists. Screen stabilization
cannot repair outlines that change shape inside an otherwise fixed frame.

## Map moments, not subjects

Map new shots to reference intervals by purpose: an opening hook, comparison,
explanation, question, evidence reveal or consequence. Relative progress can
help compare entrances with different durations. Record the mapping; do not
force equal scene counts, cast, facts or runtimes.

For each transferred motion, record its trigger, duration, amplitude, easing,
settle time and readable hold. Separate these parameters from factual values.
A numerical entrance must settle to the supported value; intermediate display
states must not look like additional measurements. Charts share the declared
scale and units throughout their animation.

Recompose for a new aspect ratio. Preserve the subject or action, protect the
text region and keep necessary context visible. A central crop is acceptable
only when the actual selected range retains that information. Reference-person
likeness, a prior cast or a prior brand is not part of the reusable style.

## Compare the delivered experience

Inspect an early representative still, then consecutive motion around entrances,
holds and cuts. Repeat against the encoded export. Compare subject hierarchy,
readability, material, timing and sound as separate dimensions; a palette match
does not establish motion fidelity.

Record observable differences and their reason. Exhaustive frame decoding,
thumbnail inspection, native-size inspection, playback and listening are
different evidence. Do not convert image statistics into an artistic similarity
score. Follow [the review method](REVIEW.md) for coverage and version identity.
