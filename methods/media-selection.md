# Select and reframe media for its role

Choose recorded video, stills, drawings, generated imagery, diagrams or mixtures
according to the brief. This method does not require footage in every film.
When the brief specifies a primary medium, make that requirement explicit in
the edit and verify it after rendering.

## Inspect the used interval

A search result, filename or clip title does not establish what is visible.
Inspect the proposed in/out interval and its crop before accepting it. Check:

- The intended subject is visible, identifiable and doing the relevant action.
- A speaker, host, cutaway or unrelated opening has not replaced the subject.
- The interval preserves the action's setup, contact and result where needed.
- Existing captions, watermarks, graphics and framing will interact correctly
  with the new composition. Preserve required attribution and licensing marks.
- Source playback speed, aspect ratio and resolution support the intended use.

Store original files unchanged. Record source identity, creator, URL or supplied
file, capture/publication context, permission status and content hash. Preserve
unknown or review-required status; availability is not a new license grant.
Keep extracted frames and crops linked to the source hash and transform settings.

## Preserve meaning through the edit

Distinguish current evidence, archive, illustration and simulation where the
viewer could confuse them. Footage accompanying a claim is not automatically
evidence of that claim. A demonstration's speed or limited scope remains visible
or audible when material to the conclusion. Recompute source attribution after
trims, substitutions, freezes and retiming.

Keep an edit map with source in/out times, output frame intervals, crop/fit,
speed, freeze remapping and overlay intervals. For variable-frame-rate inputs,
retain the source timestamp mapping or normalize with a documented transform;
do not infer source time from an assumed frame rate.

## Compose around the subject

Choose crop, contain, split layout or a contextual background based on the action.
Keep faces, hands, instruments and interacting objects visible when they carry
the meaning. Check the entire used interval because the subject may move.

Measure caption and label bounds from the actual font and text. Protect the
subject as well as the platform's text-safe region. Keep the explanation legible
without letting panels hide the main action for most of the shot. Actual source
documents retain their aspect ratio and enough context to support the claim.

## Verify the primary medium honestly

If the brief calls for a medium to dominate, report separate measurements:

| Measure | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Underlying media intervals | Which source supplies the base picture over time |
| Freeze/hold intervals | When the underlying picture is intentionally static |
| Overlay intervals | How long documents, diagrams or other panels cover it |
| Inspected encoded frames and motion | What is actually visible and readable |

Timeline occupancy is not visible pixel area or visual quality. A moving base
hidden by an opaque panel cannot alone prove a footage-led experience. Record
the metric's definition; set any threshold from the current brief, not a previous
film. Follow [reference adaptation](reference-adaptation.md) and
[review](REVIEW.md) for the visual decision.
