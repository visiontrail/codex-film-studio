## Paper, print and photographic edges

The reference combines visibly different substrates rather than applying one paper texture to every object. Frame 428 (17.12 s) combines coarse white paper, printed dots, translucent photographic glass, botanical illustration and a sculpture cutout. Frame 1514 (60.56 s) combines an ink portrait, music/newsprint, fabric-like material and charcoal lines. Frame 4320 (172.80 s) is a macro composition of corrugated cardboard, coarse red surface, dark fiber paper and irregular white torn fibers.

Current shots 04/18/46 use botanical/photo title assemblages; 16/29/39 use magenta print; 09/28/35/40/44 make a physical paper surface dominant. The remaining gap is material range: many cream strips share a similar zigzag edge, and many photographic objects share a pale sticker-like outline. A macro view of genuinely different material changes the scene more than adding another small decorative object.

## Motion: short actions, then deliberate holds

The reference frequently builds a scene in a short burst and then holds it. Examples include botanical assembly at frames 417–437 followed by a hold; a long triptych hold through approximately frame 852; and the Picasso portrait established around frame 1468, then held until the material change near 1569. Constant drift is not necessary to reproduce this rhythm.

Important motion is often local and consequential:

- **221–270 (8.84–10.80 s):** an existing head opens and a brain appears while the next portrait joins the composition.
- **1635–1670 (65.40–66.80 s):** an airplane crosses the scene as the hat exits.
- **1700–1770 (68.00–70.80 s):** individual Dada letters change paper, case and form in discrete steps.
- **2069–2138 (82.76–85.52 s):** a paper ball crosses a portrait; map and crowd take over while foreground continuity is preserved.
- **2533–2570 (101.32–102.80 s):** the calendar exits while flower/head/body pieces assemble into another subject.

The current v6 policy limits gentle global push-ins to shots 04/18/23/46. It does not apply a continuous zoom to every shot. Shot 43 visibly removes the worked answer and hint while retaining the learner and a new work surface. Its partly cropped midpoint label is an intentional exit: the full label is visible earlier and gone later.

The v5 shots 36–41 held a similar teal/strip/hand vocabulary for 73.84 seconds. Integrated v6 shot 39 now changes to magenta print for error correction; shot 40 changes to a close notebook and physically covers the solution before the reconstruction prompt. Six-phase images confirm these changes and the correct algebra: `2x + 3` is marked as the erroneous expansion and `2x + 6` is shown as the correction.

The main remaining motion gap is how often objects themselves connect or transform between scenes. Selected frames and source inspection cannot establish equivalent movement speed, easing or viewing rhythm; the encoded film still needs direct review.

## Scope of the comparison

The reference explains collage art in 3:42. This film explains AI-assisted learning through multiple studies, limitations and practical examples, over a much longer duration. It needs research figures and readable mathematical steps that the reference does not contain. The map therefore compares visual functions and material roles, with repeated reference intervals where appropriate. Normalized interval pairing is an inspection aid, not temporal, semantic or pixel equality.

The current result is recognizably an artistic collage explainer, with improved scale, layering and composition changes. Material diversity, photographic asset reuse, text dependence and cross-scene object continuity still differ visibly from the reference. These observations do not support a claim of 100% style or effect equivalence.
