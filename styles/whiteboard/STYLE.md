# Whiteboard Explainer

One physical board holds the whole argument. A marker reveals each contour;
the camera discovers connections rather than replacing the scene with slides.

Use an off-white surface with faint erased ink, single-line handwriting,
slightly uneven dry strokes and white-barrel markers. Keep the nib on the mark
while writing. Lift the pen before moving away, changing its shadow and blur.
Black carries explanation, blue supporting work and warm orange independent
attempts or emphasis. Use other colors when an object requires them, such as
the red field of a Turkish flag.

Draw people in story-specific poses: typing, writing alone or thinking with a
notebook. Include hands, the relevant tool and enough furniture to make the
action clear. Reuse a character's proportions across related shots. Draw
books, laptops, pages, flags and calendars with the same marker engine.

Keep visible ink fixed in board coordinates. Place each chapter on the same
surface. Move the camera to a relationship, hold while it is explained, and
revisit earlier drawings when useful. Leave room for subtitles. End with a
wide view that reveals how the board connects; detailed reading happens in
the closer shots.

One physical pen has one chronological stroke queue. The eraser follows
overlapping passes and leaves a faint trace; it never removes a whole rectangle
at once. Music stays quiet under narration. Felt strokes, taps and erasing
follow the same events as the picture.

Change the subject, people, layout and pacing. Preserve physical marker
behavior and the persistent board. Avoid slide transitions, typing effects,
generic icon grids and dense writing without reading time.

The included sample is a 3-minute film and a 12-minute edition of the same
story. Their renderer is independent. The visual reference is LemoLab's
*Einstein in Your Pocket*, credited in the studio's `CREDITS.md`.
