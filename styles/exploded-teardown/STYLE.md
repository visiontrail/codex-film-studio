# Exploded Teardown

A dark-studio object film. Begin with a tangible whole, separate its functional layers, visit the important interfaces, then reassemble the object. Explain how parts cooperate rather than listing components.

## Visual grammar

- Graphite field, restrained vignette and generous negative space. Warm broad key light, cooler rim and low ambient fill reveal edges without washing out the background.
- Model real thickness, mating surfaces, recesses, fasteners and supported components. Use source geometry when available and explicitly mark illustrative hardware.
- Distinguish metals, polymer, board laminate, solder mask and optics through roughness and specular response. Do not substitute a flat palette for material behavior.
- Separate layers along a coherent assembly axis. Stagger the lift from top to bottom and settle the return from bottom to top. Maintain the position of the whole while showing each part's role.
- Wide overview → close material view → functional detail → wide recombination. Plan outside camera routes with clearance; inspect the entire interpolated path for clipping and occlusion.
- Inter headings, JetBrains Mono small annotations, warm-white text and restrained orange indices. Thin projected leaders terminate at actual components; move or fade the label if it leaves the frame.
- Keep captions in a separate bottom safe area. Labels should remain readable throughout their hold, not only on a selected frame.
- Use gentle depth of field and restrained bloom. Preserve fine geometry and text after export; supersample when useful, then inspect the encoded output.

## Motion and sound

Use one frame-based timeline for transforms, camera, labels, narration, captions and sound. Let the viewer understand an assembled state before moving. Use small mechanical clicks, hinge motion and separation cues with a restrained score; duck music under speech. A fast sweep should lead to a stable view.

## Production and review

First document the object's source, dimensional assumptions and intended audience. Distinguish engineering evidence from illustrative geometry. Create the new narration and object in an independent production; do not inherit a sample's claims, branding or story.

Decode every final frame and retain a time-indexed ledger. Inspect consecutive frames, native-resolution detail, camera transitions and actual playback. Record thumbnail dimensions and exact review coverage. When comparing different subjects, map shots by function and relative phase; pixel differences are not an artistic similarity percentage. Review sound separately from ASR and technical loudness checks.

The starter supplies an empty authoring contract and shared review workflow. It does not contain a scene renderer. Published sample media is supplied separately; the public MP4s are not source CAD or complete editable productions.
