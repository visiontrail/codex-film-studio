# Newsreel

A period newsreel made from archival pictures, printed headlines, paper montages, a brisk announcer and purposeful mechanical transitions.

## Visual contract

- Use a rounded, narrow film gate within a widescreen delivery. Warm monochrome, soft vignette, restrained exposure flicker and weave unify the sources; preserve legibility.
- Headlines are printed objects: spin and settle the paper, keep the image attached, then move the camera into it. Contact shadows belong to their paper.
- A nine-card montage uses portrait cards in a 5 + 4 arrangement. Deal them in sequence, then enlarge the first card into the flipbook without a position jump. The studied rhythm is 2.7 seconds for the deal and 2.1 seconds for the flipbook; adapt only with a deliberate timing decision.
- Turn documents around the left spine with the incoming sheet underneath. Preserve occlusion and shadow through the edge-on frame.
- Use iris, blinds, clock wipes, split views and freeze-frame pencil marks when they explain a change. Do not use every transition indiscriminately.
- Pair display lettering with condensed subtitles. Leave a dark lower gradient and safe space for the requested subtitle language and line count. Check the actual font's glyph coverage.

## Sources, people and sound

Use relevant images with recorded provenance. Keep dates, sources and documentary/staged distinctions explicit. Preserve visible marks and flags when relevant; do not invent historical evidence. For a named public person in a new production, follow the repository portrait-input rule. No previous film's cast is part of the reusable style.

Write new narration. An energetic period voice can use narrow bandwidth, gentle saturation and short echo, but clarity takes priority. Write or license the score. Use one integer-frame timeline for camera, voice, captions, paper clicks and projector texture.

## Adaptation and review

Create fresh narration and visual inputs for the new subject. Inspect corresponding effect phases, not global timestamps of unrelated stories. Decode every delivery frame and inspect consecutive frames for continuity; enlarge typography, paper edges, page hinges and contact points. Check playback and listening separately. Record the resolution and scope of real observations. A successful render or ASR match does not demonstrate style equivalence.

The package is an authoring starter. It includes an empty contract and production/review guidance. The page's interactive study demonstrates a subset of effects and is not a general film renderer. A new production still requires its own scene renderer, source treatment and audio pipeline.
